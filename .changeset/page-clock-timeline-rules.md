Make a controlled page clock follow Playwright's clock. `adjust` and a forward `setTime` let the page's queued tasks run after each timer they fire, waiting while the page keeps posting messages, to a port or to its window, up to the operation's deadline, so a timer set at the end of a chain of the document's own messages fires within the same call. It doesn't wait for work in other documents, workers, or the network; a timer set then with no delay waits 1 ms. Each timer now costs real time, so a long `adjust` over a frequent interval can outlast the deadline, and a page that posts messages without end makes the operation fail with `Timeout` once its timers have run. A timer that throws is still reported to the page, and the other timers still run, but the operation now fails with `PageClockError` whose reason is the new `EvaluationThrown`. `adjust` rounds a fractional duration up to a whole millisecond and rejects negative durations, and ones that aren't finite or have a part that isn't, such as `{ milliseconds: NaN }`, with `InvalidInput`; an `offset` clock's `by` is truncated toward zero to a whole millisecond, and one that isn't finite is rejected the same way. On a controlled clock, `performance.now()` counts from 0 where the clock reaches a document, `performance.timeOrigin` is the clock's time then, and after `resume` `performance.now()`, and the times animation frames receive, carry on from where it was. Installing a controlled clock in a page whose earlier clock was removed now takes effect in its open documents.
